Rules Reference

CyberNetDeck Rules

CyberNetDeck is a two-player game where each player builds a 20–30 card deck, sets an ordered program, and lets the deck battle automatically.

Winning the battle

You can win in either of two ways:

  • Reach 20 Flux. Flux is your score and starts at 0.
  • Reduce your opponent's Sync to 0. Sync starts at 20.

Trying to draw from an empty deck loses the battle. A battle can also end at the configured action limit; the player with fewer attributed actions wins, or the battle is a draw if the counts are tied.

Reactions and triggered card effects resolve before the game checks for a winning or losing state caused by that event.

Decks and cards

Each deck contains 20–30 cards and may include any number of each card in the current format. A card with Bandwidth N costs N Bandwidth to play. A card with Sync cost N reduces its controller's Sync by N when played. A card with Ki cost N spends N Ki to play or activate; minimum Ki N is only a threshold and does not spend Ki.

Card types and properties

  • Pulse cards resolve their effects and then go to the discard pile.
  • Daemon cards remain on the battlefield with their ongoing effects active.
  • Agent cards remain on the battlefield with a printed Integrity value. Agents take damage from attacks; an Agent at 0 Integrity is deleted.
  • Named Agent: An Agent named `Name, Title` is Named, using the text before the comma as its Name. Named Agents with the same Name may coexist. This applies to ordinary and forced plays; all other play restrictions still apply.
  • Signature cards cannot be included in decks. Agents can have multiple Signatures; a granted Signature is added to the Agent's existing Signatures. An Agent may supply each named Signature card to your hand during your End phase; if that Signature is already in your hand, no additional copy is created. Multiple providers do not create duplicates in the same hand. It then plays as a normal card. Signature cards are available in the program builder when a deck card supplies them.
  • Immutable cards are limited to one copy, searched into the opening hand before ordinary draws, and played onto the battlefield before the first turn without activating their effects. They cannot be removed from the battlefield or targeted by effects.
  • Glitch cards can be played from the hand on either turn as a reaction to a specified event, then go directly to the discard pile. A Glitch Jutsu is first played as a Prepared Jutsu; after its Signs are formed, it moves to the controller's Armed Jutsu zone and waits for its reaction window. Armed Jutsu costs are paid when prepared and are not paid again when the reaction resolves. Glitches are not deleted; deletion moves a card from the battlefield to its discard pile.
  • Mission cards enter the battlefield and remain there while their Goal is active. A player may control only one Mission at a time. An attempt to add or play a Mission fails if another copy of that Mission is already in that player's hand or on that player's battlefield, or if that player has completed a copy of that Mission this battle. Each Mission name can be played only once per battle. Missions do not prepare beneath Ninjutsu. At the start of its controller's turn, after the Draw phase, a Mission checks its Goal and requirement; a completed Mission resolves its Complete instruction and is deleted.

Card zones and movement

Delete moves a card from its controller's battlefield to that player's Discard; it does not resolve that card again. Wipe moves a card to the separate Void zone. The Void is not the Discard, so effects that target the Discard cannot target cards in the Void.

When an effect says a player discards a card at random, one card instance is chosen from that player's hand and moved to that player's Discard. If that player's hand is empty, nothing is discarded.

Missions and Jutsu

An ordinary Jutsu can be played only as a Prepared Jutsu. A Prepared Jutsu is a Jutsu placed face up beneath the card that prepared it after its non-Sign costs are paid. It remains on its controller's board as an attached card rather than occupying a free-standing battlefield position. It is public, cannot be affected by other cards, and only one Prepared Jutsu can be held at a time.

Signs are a sequence that must be formed in order, over any number of turns, before the Prepared Jutsu can resolve. Whenever you play a non-Jutsu card, form the next Sign. The card's type can form one additional matching Sign, first if able and otherwise second:

  • An Agent forms Ram.
  • A Daemon forms Ox.
  • A Pulse forms Tiger.
  • A Glitch forms Snake.

A played card can provide at most one additional Sign. Starting a Jutsu is a card play but does not form a Sign; forming a Sign is not playing a card.

When all Signs are formed, resolve the Prepared Jutsu without paying its costs again. A Jutsu with no Signs cost resolves immediately after it is prepared; an ordinary Jutsu with Signs resolves after its final Sign. A Pulse Jutsu then goes to its controller's Discard. A Daemon Jutsu Module remains on the battlefield and attaches to its chosen Agent. A Glitch Jutsu moves to Armed Jutsu after its Signs are formed, then is discarded after its reaction resolves.

Ninjutsu lets you place one Jutsu as a Prepared Jutsu instead of resolving it, after paying its non-Sign costs.

A Mission's Goal N advances by 1 at the start of its controller's turn, after the Draw phase, up to its printed value. Then check whether its Goal and printed requirement are complete. On completion, first apply any stated persistent Signature grant; this changes the Agent's future End-phase Signature abilities but does not create a card in hand. Then resolve its Complete instruction and delete the Mission. Agents and Daemons entering play do not advance a Mission's Goal.

Armed Jutsu is public and holds a controller's prepared Glitch Jutsu. It does not count as a Prepared Jutsu beneath Ninjutsu and does not contribute to Signs. A reaction from Armed Jutsu is played without another cost payment.

Daemon Jutsu Modules attach to the highest-Breach controlled Agent, breaking ties by oldest battlefield instance. If a Module's host would be chosen to take damage and another Agent can take that damage, the host is ignored and the Module is deleted. If no Agent exists when the Module resolves, the Module deletes itself. If its attached Agent is successfully deleted, the Module is deleted too; if that deletion is prevented, the Module remains.

Damage targeting

Damage is dealt to the opposing player. You do not choose a particular Agent; the game applies the following order every time an effect deals damage.

  1. No opposing Agents: the damage reduces that player's Sync.
  2. An Agent can be destroyed: if one or more opposing Agents have remaining Integrity equal to or less than the damage, the game chooses at random among only those destroyable Agents. It damages that Agent and deletes it at 0 Integrity.
  3. No Agent can be destroyed: the game chooses an Agent with the lowest remaining Integrity. If several Agents are tied for lowest, it chooses at random among those tied Agents. That Agent takes all of the damage.

An Agent absorbs the entire damage event. Damage never carries over from that Agent to another Agent or to Sync, even if the Agent is deleted.

Direct Integrity loss

Direct Integrity loss is separate from damage. An effect that makes your Agents lose Integrity applies independently to every eligible Agent you control, in battlefield order. Immutable cards are not eligible. Each Agent loses up to the stated amount, based on its remaining Integrity, and an Agent at 0 Integrity is deleted to Discard. The loss never chooses randomly, spills to another Agent or to Sync, or triggers Sync-loss amplification. If the effect grants Flux for Integrity lost this way, it grants Flux equal to the total actual loss after all eligible Agents are processed.

Battle flow

Each player starts with five cards. Ki, Uplink, and current Bandwidth all start at 0; Bandwidth's maximum also starts at 0. Turns proceed through Start, Draw, Main, and End.

  • Start: The turn begins and its player gains 1 Uplink. Their Bandwidth maximum increases by 1, and their current Bandwidth is restored to that new maximum. Uplink requirements are thresholds, not costs.
  • Draw: Draw one card. Drawing from an empty deck loses the battle.
  • Main: Follow the program while playable cards remain in hand.
  • End: End-phase effects resolve in their normal order. The turn then ends and the other player begins their turn.

Bandwidth is a spendable resource. Playing a card with Bandwidth N reduces your current Bandwidth by N; it cannot be spent below 0. Unspent Bandwidth does not carry through the next Start phase. Programs can compare your current Bandwidth with the quantity condition; that condition does not compare the maximum.

When an effect forces a player to play the top card of their deck, the card still pays all of its listed Flux, Bandwidth, and Sync costs. If its costs cannot be paid, it goes to that player's Discard without resolving. Other play restrictions retain their normal behavior.

Sync costs reduce your current Sync when the card is played. You cannot pay more Sync than you have. For a reaction with a cost, the game confirms that the cost can be paid, announces the reaction, then pays the cost before the card moves or resolves. If paying a Sync cost reaches 0 Sync, zero-Sync effects resolve before the loss check; if Sync remains 0, the battle ends before the card moves or resolves.

Programs

An Action program is an ordered list of rules. During Main, the rules are checked from top to bottom. The first rule whose condition is true and whose action can be performed is used.

`A card is...` conditions can check whether a named card is in your hand, on your side of the board, or on your opponent's side of the board. A public Prepared Jutsu beneath its preparing card is on that player's board too; it cannot be affected by other cards while it is Prepared. Quantity conditions first choose Your or Opponent's, then compare Cards in deck, Cards in hand, Cards on board, Flux, Ki, Bandwidth, or Sync using fewer than, at most, exactly, at least, or more than a whole-number threshold. Board counts include cards in the selected player's battlefield and public Prepared Jutsu area. Bandwidth compares current spendable Bandwidth, not its maximum.

Play a Combo is an ordered list of named card choices. By default, the engine attempts those choices from first to last even when some listed card types are absent from your hand. Enable Only when all cards in hand to make the Combo a non-match unless at least one copy of every listed card type is in your hand; programming then moves to the next condition/action. Unavailable choices are skipped by default. A choice marked (all) with the infinity marker is attempted repeatedly while another copy is in hand and legally playable; an unmarked choice is attempted once. Each card uses its normal play timing, costs, restrictions, and effects; the Combo is programming metadata only and does not appear as a separate battle event or log entry. The optional Cancel if card isn't played setting stops the Combo without trying later choices when its current choice cannot be played before any copy of that card type succeeds. If the Combo plays no card at all, it is a non-match and programming checks the next available Action rule, regardless of either option. If an (all) choice plays at least one copy, a later copy that cannot play does not cancel the Combo; the next sequence choice is tried. Both options are off by default.

Actions can play a named card, play a Combo, activate an Immutable card, play a random card, or end the turn. If no Action rule is selected, the default action is used. When your deck contains a card that can add another named card to your deck, the added card is also available in the condition and named-action selectors; it still cannot be played until a copy is actually in your hand.

An Agent's named Signature is also available in those selectors, even though it cannot be added to the deck. It becomes playable only after the Agent supplies a fresh copy to your hand. The Combo card picker uses this same deck-derived availability set, showing the cards while they are unselected and numbering them in the order you select them.

Reactions

A Reaction program is separate from the Action program and has no default action. Whenever an event in the game occurs, the Reaction rules are checked in order. The current turn player gets priority first, followed by the other player, alternating until both players have no eligible Reaction to the event. The first eligible Glitch for the player with priority is played. If no reaction is eligible or its costs cannot be paid, nothing happens.

A reaction window belongs to one pending event. If a reaction prevents that event, the reaction window closes immediately; no other matching Glitch can be played for that same event. If a reaction resolves without preventing the event and the event remains pending, priority continues to alternate until both players pass.

Simulations and battle logs

Simulations play the selected number of battles against each available opponent. Select a matchup and then a battle to read its player-facing log.

Current backlog cards and format updates

If an Action program has no selected action, its default action is End the turn. A program that explicitly chooses Play a random card still uses the random-card behavior; the omission is what now defaults to ending the turn.

Immutable is the fifth card kind. Immutables use the Immutable marker and are not Agents or Daemons for effects that target those kinds. Ninjutsu and Charge Ki are Immutables. Crush Card Virus destroys every Agent and Daemon on both battlefields while leaving Immutables untouched.
