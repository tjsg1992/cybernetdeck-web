Rules Reference

CyberNetDeck Rules

CyberNetDeck is a two-player game in which each player builds a 20–30 card deck and programs how it plays.

Winning the battle

You win by reaching 20 Flux, or by reducing your opponent's Sync to 0. Flux starts at 0 and Sync starts at 20. Reactions and triggered effects finish resolving before the game checks the result of the event.

Drawing from an empty deck loses the battle. A configured action limit can also end a battle; the player with fewer attributed actions wins, or the battle is a draw when the counts are tied.

Decks, costs, and card types

Each deck contains 20–30 cards and may include any number of each card in the format. Bandwidth N costs N Bandwidth. Sync cost N reduces Sync by N. Ki cost N spends Ki; minimum Ki N only checks that you have enough Ki.

  • Pulse: resolves, then goes to its controller's Discard.
  • Daemon: remains on the battlefield after resolving.
  • Agent: remains on the battlefield and has Integrity. An Agent at 0 Integrity is deleted.
  • Immutable: is limited to one copy, starts in your opening hand, and is played before the first turn. It cannot be targeted or removed.
  • Glitch: is played from hand as a reaction to its specified event, then goes to Discard.

An Agent with a name in the form `Name, Title` is a Named Agent. Named Agents with the same Name may coexist. A Signature cannot be included in a deck; an Agent may have more than one Signature, and a supplied Signature is added to the Agent's existing Signatures. A fresh Signature card is supplied to its controller's hand during End and is then played normally.

Breach N makes an Agent deal N damage during its controller's opponent's End phase. Damage targets opposing Agents first, then Sync if no Agent remains.

Zones and movement

Cards can be in a deck, hand, battlefield, Prepared Jutsu area, Armed Jutsu area, Discard, or Void. Delete moves a battlefield card to its controller's Discard without resolving it again. Wipe moves a card to the Void. The Void is separate from Discard.

Random discard chooses one card instance from the player's hand. If the hand is empty, nothing is discarded.

Jutsu

A Jutsu is played as a Prepared Jutsu. Pay its non-Sign costs when it is prepared and place it face up in the public Prepared Jutsu area. Only one Prepared Jutsu can be held at a time. It does not occupy a normal battlefield position and cannot be affected by other cards while prepared.

Signs

Signs are formed in order over any number of turns. Whenever you play a non-Jutsu card, form the next Sign. That card can also form one matching Sign, first if possible and otherwise second:

  • Agent — Ram
  • Daemon — Ox
  • Pulse — Tiger
  • Glitch — Snake

Starting a Jutsu is a card play but does not form a Sign. Forming a Sign is not playing a card. A Jutsu with no Signs resolves immediately after it is prepared.

Resolving Jutsu

When the final Sign is formed, resolve the Prepared Jutsu without paying its costs again. A resolving Pulse Jutsu goes to Discard. A Jutsu that is also a Module enters the battlefield and attaches as described below.

Armed Jutsu

A Glitch Jutsu moves to the public Armed Jutsu area when its Sign sequence is complete. If it has no Signs, it becomes Armed immediately after it is prepared. It waits there until its reaction condition occurs. Resolving an Armed Jutsu does not pay its costs again; afterward it goes to Discard. Armed Jutsu do not occupy the battlefield or form Signs.

Missions

You may control only one Mission. Adding or playing a Mission fails if another copy is already in your hand or on your battlefield, or if you have completed that Mission this battle. Each Mission name can be played only once per battle.

At the start of your turn, after you draw, each Mission you control advances its Goal by 1 up to its printed value and then checks its requirement. If the requirement is met, resolve its Complete instruction and delete it. A persistent Signature reward changes future End-phase supplies; it does not put a card in your hand at that moment.

Modules

A Module is a Daemon subtype that attaches to a card when it enters play. It stays on the battlefield until its effect deletes it. If its attached Agent is deleted, the Module is deleted too. If the Agent's deletion is prevented, the Module remains attached.

Damage targeting

Damage is dealt to the opposing player. You do not choose a particular Agent; the game applies the following order every time an effect deals damage.

  1. No opposing Agents: the damage reduces that player's Sync.
  2. An Agent can be destroyed: if one or more opposing Agents have remaining Integrity equal to or less than the damage, the game chooses at random among only those destroyable Agents. It damages that Agent and deletes it at 0 Integrity.
  3. No Agent can be destroyed: the game chooses an Agent with the lowest remaining Integrity. If several Agents are tied for lowest, it chooses at random among those tied Agents. That Agent takes all of the damage.

An Agent absorbs the entire damage event. Damage never carries over from that Agent to another Agent or to Sync, even if the Agent is deleted.

Direct Integrity loss

Direct Integrity loss is separate from damage. An effect that makes your Agents lose Integrity applies independently to every eligible Agent you control, in battlefield order. Immutable cards are not eligible. Each Agent loses up to the stated amount, based on its remaining Integrity, and an Agent at 0 Integrity is deleted to Discard. The loss never chooses randomly, spills to another Agent or to Sync, or triggers Sync-loss amplification. If the effect grants Flux for Integrity lost this way, it grants Flux equal to the total actual loss after all eligible Agents are processed.

Battle flow

Each player starts with five cards. Ki, Uplink, and current Bandwidth all start at 0; Bandwidth's maximum also starts at 0. Turns proceed through Start, Draw, Main, and End.

  • Start: The turn begins and its player gains 1 Uplink. Their Bandwidth maximum increases by 1, and their current Bandwidth is restored to that new maximum. Uplink requirements are thresholds, not costs.
  • Draw: Draw one card. Drawing from an empty deck loses the battle.
  • Main: Follow the program while playable cards remain in hand.
  • End: End-phase effects resolve in their normal order. The turn then ends and the other player begins their turn.

Cards pay their listed costs when they are played, including reactions and forced plays. A forced play that cannot pay its costs goes to Discard without resolving. When an effect forces a player to play the top card of their deck, that card follows its normal play restrictions and costs. Unspent Bandwidth does not carry through Start. If a Sync payment reaches 0, zero-Sync effects resolve before the battle checks for a loss.

Programs

An Action program is an ordered list of rules. During Main, the first rule whose condition is true and whose action can be performed is used. Actions can play a named card, play a Combo, activate an Immutable, play a random card, or end the turn. If no rule is selected, the default action is used.

Conditions can check whether a named card is in hand or on either side of the board. A public Prepared Jutsu beneath its preparing card is on that player's board too. Quantity conditions compare Cards in deck, Cards in hand, Cards on board, Flux, Ki, Bandwidth, or Sync for Your or Opponent's player.

Programming choices include every card in your deck and every card reachable through its add-to-deck results, named Signatures, and eligible Mission Signatures. This does not create a card or bypass normal costs, timing, or zone restrictions; a Signature must still be supplied to your hand before it can be played.

Play a Combo is an ordered list of named cards. Unavailable choices are skipped by default and each choice is attempted once. Mark a choice (all) to repeat it while another playable copy is available. Only when all cards in hand requires every listed card to be in hand. Cancel if card isn't played stops when the current choice cannot be played. A Combo uses each card's normal costs, timing, and restrictions.

Reactions

A Reaction program is separate from the Action program and has no default action. Whenever an event in the game occurs, the Reaction rules are checked in order. The current turn player gets priority first, followed by the other player, alternating until both players have no eligible Reaction to the event. The first eligible Glitch for the player with priority is played. If no reaction is eligible or its costs cannot be paid, nothing happens.

A reaction window belongs to one pending event. If a reaction prevents that event, the reaction window closes immediately; no other matching Glitch can be played for that same event. If a reaction resolves without preventing the event and the event remains pending, priority continues to alternate until both players pass.
